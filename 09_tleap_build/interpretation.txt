# 09_tleap_build — Topology build with ff14SB, GAFF and TIP3P

## What this step did
step09a combined the H++-protonated protein with the three frozen substrates into one
pre-tleap PDB, deriving every count from the inputs rather than hard-coding it. step09b
built the topology with tleap, solvated in a 10 A box, neutralised with Na+, and audited
both the topology and the tleap log.

## Key numbers
- Pre-tleap: 6183 protein atoms + 72 substrate atoms = 6255 combined, 384 residues.
- Charge: protein -3, three substrates -6, complex -9, neutralised with 9 Na+ and 0 Cl-.
- Dry topology: 6255 atoms, 384 residues, charge -9, no box.
- Solvated: 55680 atoms, 16865 residues, 16472 waters, 9 Na+, charge 0.000, box
  92.915 x 76.664 x 95.443 A.
- Build recipe: leaprc.protein.ff14SB + leaprc.gaff + leaprc.water.tip3p, solvatebox
  TIP3PBOX 10.0, addions Na+ 0.
- tleap: 28 warnings, 0 errors, 0 fatals. Buckets: 12 terminal renames, 2 non-zero dry
  charge, 1 unit-check note, 14 close-contact reports over 7 distinct pairs, 0 unclassified.

## Acceptance threshold and verdict
Hard gates are structural only: substrate template not -2, non-integer charge, HIS left
un-renamed, duplicate atom names, chain gaps, a real clash, or atom counts changing
through assembly. The expected -3 / -9 / 9 charge values are computed and REVIEW-flagged
if they differ rather than being fatal, which keeps the step portable. Review field: none.
Verification: 36 checks, 36 PASS, 0 FAIL. See checks/step09_verify.out.

## THE FINDING: where the seven close contacts actually sit
The audit records 7 distinct close contacts, the tightest at 1.317 A, and classes them as
"all solute-internal ... relaxed during restrained minimisation". That classification is
accurate but it is not the whole story. Resolving each pair against the graft provenance
of step 02 and the contact set of step 04:

    1.317 A  A121:LEU:CD1  <-> C70:TYR:HH     A121 GRAFTED from 1DBF
    1.375 A  B75:CYS:HG    <-> C201:CHA:H8    LIGAND + ACTIVE SITE
    1.396 A  A121:LEU:HD12 <-> C70:TYR:HH     A121 GRAFTED
    1.473 A  A121:LEU:HD13 <-> A121:LEU:HD11  A121 GRAFTED, same residue
    1.480 A  A119:LEU:HD23 <-> A119:LEU:HD22  A119 GRAFTED, same residue
    1.481 A  A119:LEU:HD22 <-> A119:LEU:HD21  A119 GRAFTED, same residue
    1.487 A  A121:LEU:HD11 <-> C70:TYR:HH     A121 GRAFTED

SIX OF SEVEN INVOLVE RESIDUES GRAFTED FROM 1DBF AT STEP 02. A119 and A121 both sit inside
chain A's 116-127 patch; C70 is native 2CHT. So the worst steric problems in the built
system are at the graft, between donor-derived and scaffold-derived residues. This is the
same graft that step 02 already flagged for a post-minimisation re-check, and it
strengthens that item considerably: the commitment is not only about C-N join distances
but about steric relief.

THE SEVENTH IS AT THE ACTIVE SITE, AND IT HAS A CAUSE. B75:CYS:HG to C201:CHA:H8 at
1.375 A. Cys75' is a contact-set residue, measured at 3.188 A heavy-atom to cha_c in step
04. HG is the cysteine thiol hydrogen, placed by H++.

step07a submitted a PROTEIN-ONLY structure: hpp_input_protein_only_with_TER.pdb contains
0 HETATM records. H++ therefore placed the Cys75 thiol hydrogen with no substrate present
in the structure, and it landed 1.375 A from a substrate hydrogen. The clash is a direct
and predictable consequence of the protein-only submission, not a defect in either tool.

Whether it matters depends entirely on whether restrained minimisation relieves it. That
is exactly what step 10 must show, and no committed output shows it yet.

## Literature comparison
THE BUILD REPRODUCES AGBAGLO'S PUBLISHED PROTOCOL. It is not a deviation from anything.

Agbaglo et al. 2024, main text p. 7:
  "The AMBER18 MD package was used to run the MD simulations, and the AMBER force field
   ff14SB was used with periodic boundary conditions and a cutoff value of 9 A for
   non-bonded interactions. The Antechamber package was employed to parameterize the
   chorismate substrate with the Generalized Amber Force Field (GAFF). The protonated
   structure with chorismate was solvated in a cubic 10 A box of water with the explicit
   solvent model TIP3P. The MD model charge was neutralized by adding 9 Na+ ions."

Element by element against the committed tleap_complex.in and step09b_topology_audit.txt:

    protein force field   ff14SB              leaprc.protein.ff14SB
    substrate             Antechamber + GAFF  leaprc.gaff, GAFF selected explicitly
    water                 TIP3P               leaprc.water.tip3p
    box                   cubic, 10 A         solvatebox TIP3PBOX 10.0
    neutralisation        9 Na+               addions Na+ 0 -> 9 Na+, 0 Cl-, charge 0.000
    production            20 ns               step10c, 20 ns

Matching to the ion count is not coincidence. The step09 method note calling this
"paper-aligned" is accurate, and the paper it aligns to is Agbaglo, not Claeyssens.

THE PATTERN ACROSS PHASE 1, which should be stated once in the methods rather than
re-argued at each step:
  The MD SYSTEM BUILD follows Agbaglo et al. 2024 throughout - the 1DBF terminal repair
  with their exact residue ranges (step 02), H++ protonation (step 07), ff14SB + GAFF +
  TIP3P with a 10 A box and 9 Na+ (step 09), and a 20 ns production run (step 10c).
  The QM/MM REACTION WORK is benchmarked against Claeyssens et al. 2011.
  Claeyssens never built an AMBER system; their starting structures came from
  SCCDFTB/CHARMM22 QM/MM MD. There is therefore no CHARMM22 protocol here to depart from,
  and describing this build as a deviation from Claeyssens would be a category error.

WHAT DOES NEED STATING, and it is a consequence rather than a choice: the classical
Hamiltonian that generates our sampled ensemble is not the one Claeyssens used. The two
ensembles are therefore not drawn from the same potential, even though the QM/MM barriers
on top of them are computed at the same level of theory. That is a caveat on any
ensemble-level comparison against Claeyssens - the barrier distribution, the spread, the
regression - and not on a single-geometry barrier. Carry it into the ensemble sections.

ONE MORE POINT FROM THE SAME PASSAGE, bearing on step 07. Agbaglo add hydrogens "via the
H++ server using default parameters". Since this build follows them everywhere else, our
H++ settings were most likely the server defaults, which are consistent with the code-repo
copy of step07a and inconsistent with the archived copy. Circumstantial, not a record.
See sourcing_gaps.txt item 4, where it is filed as NARROWED and explicitly NOT closed.

A DIFFERENCE THAT IS REAL: Agbaglo ran 20 ns of production. Our step10c is 20 ns, but the
ensemble draws frames out to 43738 ps, so step10d extended beyond the reference. Confirm
and record the extension at step 10.

## Caveats and known issues
- NET CHARGE DIFFERS BETWEEN THE TWO ENVIRONMENTS BY CONSTRUCTION. The enzyme system is
  neutralised to exactly zero with 9 Na+, whereas the bare-substrate work treats a dianion
  in continuum. Expected and correct, but barriers from the two are not on the same
  electrostatic footing and must not be differenced naively.
- NO AS-RUN SCRIPT ARCHIVE EXISTS FOR STEP 09, as for step 08.
- complex_solvated.prmtop is 10.1 MB and is held as a pointer under the repository's own
  10 MB rule. Its hash is recorded in the pointer file and the verification checks that.
- prmtop files embed a wall-clock DATE line, so their raw hashes churn between otherwise
  identical builds. The step provides step09_fingerprints.txt for DATE-insensitive
  comparison; the checksum check skips raw prmtop hashes deliberately.
- The bucket counts sum to 29 against a reported total of 28 warnings. The extra is the
  unit-check note, which the classifier buckets separately from the warning stream. Not an
  error, but the two numbers are counting slightly different things.
- The solvated PDB saturates at 9999 residues, so water and ion counts must be read from
  the prmtop, never the PDB. The step's own header says this and the audit follows it.
