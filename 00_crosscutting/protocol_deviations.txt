# protocol_deviations.txt

Every place this work departs from Claeyssens et al. 2011, the primary comparator, with
the reason and the step where it originates. Written as each deviation is ENCOUNTERED
during the step-by-step audit, so this file is incomplete until the audit reaches step 19.

A deviation is not a fault. An UNSTATED deviation is.

Status key:
  RECORDED  - stated somewhere in the repos already
  UNSTATED  - found during the audit, not yet written into any script comment, note or
              draft section other than this file

===============================================================================
DEVIATION 1 - Terminal repair follows Agbaglo, not Claeyssens
  origin step: 02_terminal_repair (inputs registered at 01)
  status: UNSTATED in the repos; CORROBORATED against Agbaglo as of 2026-09-28
===============================================================================

Claeyssens 2011 Methods (p. 2-3) describe taking 2CHT, substituting chorismate for the
bound analogue, hydrogenating and solvating. They make NO mention of unresolved termini,
of repairing them, or of a donor structure.

We graft the unresolved chain termini from PDB 1DBF. So does Agbaglo et al. 2024 (p. 6),
using the same donor, specifying residues 1 and 116-127 for chain A, 1 and 115-127 for
chain B, and 1-2 and 115-127 for chain C.

So at this step we follow the secondary reference, not the primary one.

CORROBORATION. step02_verify.py section 4 reads the donor provenance of every residue in
abc_repaired.pdb from step02_residue_source_report.tsv and compares it against both the
patch definition hard-coded in the script AND the ranges published by Agbaglo. All three
chains match Agbaglo's ranges EXACTLY - 13 grafted residues in chain A, 14 in B, 15 in C,
no extras and no omissions. The agreement is residue for residue.

This is therefore not "we followed a protocol we read about". It is a reproduction of a
published repair, verified against the publication, on a choice the primary comparator
does not report at all. State it that way.

Reason for repairing at all: leaving termini unresolved would leave unphysical chain
breaks in an MD system. Claeyssens' silence is not an argument against repair - it may
simply be unreported - but it does mean the primary comparator cannot be cited for it.

Note that step02_terminal_repair_abc.sh credits "the reference MD-preparation protocol"
without naming it. See open_items.txt item 3.

DEVIATION 2 - Substrate geometry provenance differs
  origin step: 01_inputs_acquisition
  status: UNSTATED
===============================================================================

We take three pre-aligned chorismate geometries from the CP2K examples repository at
commit d71583a411a0a7d8557e94a24b87a3ceb87ec34e, each registered by nearest centre to a
specific crystallographic analogue site (K210, J212, L211).

Claeyssens substitute chorismate for the analogue "as described in references 9 and 10"
= Ranaghan et al., Mol. Phys. 2003, 101, 2695-2714, and Org. Biomol. Chem. 2004, 2,
968-980.

Neither reference is in the reference repository and no DOI is held for either, so the
procedure being deviated from CANNOT CURRENTLY BE INSPECTED. The deviation may be large
or may be nil; that is not presently knowable. See sourcing_gaps.txt.

Note that the analogue is a different molecule from the substrate: 17 heavy atoms at site
K210 against 16 in the chorismate dianion. The superposition at step 04 is therefore not
an atom-for-atom mapping, whichever procedure is used.

===============================================================================
DEVIATION 3 - The barrier is a different quantity
  origin step: spans 15 (reactant opt) through 19 (ensemble harvest)
  status: UNSTATED
===============================================================================

Recorded here early because it affects every barrier quoted anywhere in the thesis, and
because it is currently written down nowhere in either repository.

Claeyssens 2011 computes barriers and stabilisations relative to a FIXED point on the
reaction coordinate, r = -1.8 A (RS*), not relative to the reactant minimum. The minimum
sits at r = -1.6 A in the enzyme and -2.0 A in water, so a common reference was chosen to
compare the two environments. They report the shift as worth 0.1 kcal/mol in the enzyme
and 0.8 in water. Their Fig. 2 profiles use a second definition - lowest point on the
reactant side - so two definitions coexist in that paper, 0.1 kcal/mol apart in the
enzyme.

Claeyssens 2005 uses r = -1.8 A as its reactant ENDPOINT.

Ours is a third definition: converged climbing image minus a FREELY RELAXED reactant
endpoint. We are the only one of the three that lets the reactant relax.

Direction is predictable - a relaxed reactant sits lower, so our barrier is biased HIGH
relative to theirs. The MAGNITUDE IS NOT ESTABLISHED and should not be guessed. If it is
to be quantified, the cheap test is to evaluate our reactant energy at a fixed r = -1.8 A
and difference it against the relaxed value.

DEVIATION 4 - WITHDRAWN. The MD system build is not a deviation; it reproduces
              Agbaglo's published protocol.
  origin step: 09_tleap_build
  status: WITHDRAWN 2026-09-28. Entered in error, measured against the wrong paper.
===============================================================================

This was entered as "force field: ff14SB + GAFF + TIP3P, not CHARMM22", framed as a
departure from Claeyssens. That framing was wrong.

Agbaglo et al. 2024, main text p. 7:
  "The AMBER18 MD package was used to run the MD simulations, and the AMBER force field
   ff14SB was used with periodic boundary conditions and a cutoff value of 9 A for
   non-bonded interactions. The Antechamber package was employed to parameterize the
   chorismate substrate with the Generalized Amber Force Field (GAFF). The protonated
   structure with chorismate was solvated in a cubic 10 A box of water with the explicit
   solvent model TIP3P. The MD model charge was neutralized by adding 9 Na+ ions."

Our build, read from the committed tleap_complex.in and step09b_topology_audit.txt:
  leaprc.protein.ff14SB, leaprc.gaff, leaprc.water.tip3p
  solvatebox TIP3PBOX 10.0
  addions complex Na+ 0  ->  9 Na+, 0 Cl-, total charge 0.000
  20 ns production at step 10c

Every element matches, down to the ion count. This is reproduction, not deviation.

THE REAL PATTERN, and it should be stated once rather than as repeated deviations:
  The MD SYSTEM BUILD follows Agbaglo et al. 2024 throughout - terminal repair from 1DBF
  with their exact residue ranges (deviation 1), H++ protonation, ff14SB + GAFF + TIP3P,
  a 10 A box, 9 Na+, 20 ns production.
  The QM/MM REACTION WORK is benchmarked against Claeyssens et al. 2011.
  These are two different reference points for two different parts of the pipeline, and
  that is a coherent choice. Claeyssens did not build an AMBER MD system at all; they
  generated their starting structures by SCCDFTB/CHARMM22 QM/MM MD. There is no CHARMM22
  protocol here to deviate from.

What DOES still need stating in the methods is the consequence, not the choice: the
classical Hamiltonian generating our sampled ensemble is not the one Claeyssens used, so
the two ensembles are not drawn from the same potential even though the QM/MM barriers are
computed at the same level of theory. That belongs in the write-up as a caveat on the
ensemble comparison, not as a protocol deviation.

DEVIATION 5 - WITHDRAWN. The boundary treatment likewise follows Agbaglo.
  origin step: 09_tleap_build
  status: WITHDRAWN 2026-09-28, same error as deviation 4.
===============================================================================

Entered as "periodic rectangular box, not a fixed-shell sphere", framed against
Claeyssens 2005's 25 A sphere with a fixed 5 A outer shell.

Agbaglo use "periodic boundary conditions and a cutoff value of 9 A" and "a cubic 10 A box
of water". Our solvatebox TIP3PBOX 10.0 gives 92.915 x 76.664 x 95.443 A with 16472
waters. Same treatment, same buffer.

One point from the withdrawn entry SURVIVES and is worth keeping, because it is about our
own work rather than about a comparison:
  The enzyme system is neutralised to exactly zero total charge with 9 Na+, whereas the
  bare-substrate work treats a dianion in continuum. The two environments differ in net
  charge by construction. That is expected and correct, but it means barriers from the two
  are not on the same electrostatic footing and should not be differenced naively.
  -> carried to open_items.txt if it is not already covered there.

A SEPARATE AND UNRESOLVED POINT, raised by the same passage:
  Agbaglo state that hydrogens "were added to this structure via the H++ server using
  DEFAULT PARAMETERS". If our step 07 followed Agbaglo as the rest of the build does, the
  H++ settings would be the server defaults. That is consistent with the code-repo copy of
  step07a (internal dielectric 10, external 80) and inconsistent with the archived copy
  (internal 80, external 10). This is INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE, not proof, and does not close
  sourcing_gaps.txt item 4 - the notebook is still the authority. But it narrows it, and
  it should be recorded against that gap.

DEVIATION 6 - Production runs to 60 ns, three times the reference protocol
  origin step: 10_md
  status: UNSTATED
===============================================================================

Agbaglo et al. 2024 p. 7: "The protein was then allowed to move freely for a 20 ns
production-level run. The timescale of each frame was 1 ps, for a total of 20,000 frames."

Our step10c reproduces that exactly: nstlim 10000000 x dt 0.002 = 20 ns, ntwx 500 x 0.002
= 1 ps per frame, 20000 frames. Then step10d extends production by a further 40 ns, giving
60 ns in total. The canonical selection manifest runs to prod_ps 59999.

CONSEQUENCE, and it is substantial: 29 of the 44 ensemble frames lie beyond 20000 ps. TWO
THIRDS OF THE ENSEMBLE IS DRAWN FROM OUTSIDE THE WINDOW THE REFERENCE PROTOCOL COVERS.

This is a deliberate improvement, not an accident - a longer trajectory samples more
independent configurations, and the ensemble is the point of Phase 1. But it must be
stated in X.2 rather than left for a reader to infer from frame numbers, for two reasons:
  - Any comparison of our conformational sampling against Agbaglo's is not like for like.
  - The equilibration-cut decision recorded elsewhere in this project (frames before
    20000 ps treated as a pilot) interacts with this directly: the cut and the extension
    are the same boundary seen from two sides.

===============================================================================
DEVIATION 7 - The ensemble samples one active site, not the trimer
  origin step: 10_md / 12_frame_selection
  status: UNSTATED in the repos; reason supplied by the author 2026-09-28
===============================================================================

All 44 ensemble frames come from CHA#2, residue 383, site B. Sites A and C are built,
parameterised, solvated and simulated, and then never sampled for QM/MM.

REASON, as stated: computational cost, and Phase 2 examines a single-substrate reaction,
so a three-site ensemble buys nothing the design work can use. Defensible, and it should
be written into a method note since no committed file records it.

WHY IT IS A DEVIATION WORTH STATING. Claeyssens et al. 2011 calculated 16 enzyme reaction
paths and their barrier spread of +/- 1.8 kcal/mol is a spread ACROSS active-site
configurations. Our 44 frames are drawn from within ONE site. The two spreads are
therefore not the same quantity, even before the sampling-length difference of deviation 6
is considered. Any comparison of our barrier distribution against theirs - the standard
deviation, the regression scatter, the range - inherits this.

Site B is also the best-behaved of the three on every committed measure: TSA fit RMSD
0.0797 against 0.1267 and 0.1518, smallest centre offset, tightest torsion sampling
(sd 8.0 against 36.7 and 63.9), and Arg63' bound rather than swung. That makes it a
defensible representative, and it makes the ensemble spread a within-site spread from the
most ordered site available. Both halves of that should be in the write-up.
