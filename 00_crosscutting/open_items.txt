ITEM 10 - The step 11 document sets a selection criterion that was not applied
  raised at: 11_trajectory_analysis

step11_trajectory_analysis.md closes with:
    "Select catalytically-competent reactant frames ... Draws from all three sites,
     weighted toward CHA#2; gives a Claeyssens-style spread of competent conformations."

The canonical manifest 05_qmmm/selection_manifest.tsv holds 45 frames and every one is

either.

To close: correct the document to state the criterion actually applied and the occupancy
figures that justify it, and make sure X.2 says plainly that the ensemble is single-site.

ITEM 12 - NAC as a filter must not be read as NAC as a mechanism
  raised at: 11_trajectory_analysis
  closes at: a sentence in X.2
  status: OPEN

Frame selection uses the Hur & Bruice near-attack-conformer criterion (PNAS 2003, 100,
12015), correctly sourced and fully implemented in step11f_nac_check.py.

Ranaghan et al. 2004 (doi:10.1039/b313759g, held as B313759G.pdf, reference 10 of
Claeyssens 2011) sets out the controversy: Bruice's claim that ~90% of the catalytic
effect arises from NAC formation "has been criticized as being unreliable", Jorgensen's
MC/FEP study suggests NAC conversion contributes no free energy to catalysis, and the
Mulholland lineage - including our primary comparator - argues for transition-state
stabilisation instead.

Using a NAC criterion to SELECT reactant-like frames is not the same as endorsing NAC as
the EXPLANATION of catalysis. The two must be distinguished in one explicit sentence, or a
reader will take the filter as a mechanistic commitment that the rest of the thesis, and
the comparator it benchmarks against, contradicts.
