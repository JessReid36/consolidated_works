# 19_ensemble — The barrier ensemble and the Claeyssens comparison

## What this step did
Ran the QM/MM reaction path for every selected frame, harvested the barriers, computed an
in vacuo reference at each optimised geometry, and derived the differential
transition-state stabilisation. This is the quantity the whole of Phase 1 exists to
produce, and the only one directly comparable with the published work.

## Key numbers, all re-derived here rather than read
Two sub-ensembles, distinguished by frame number:

      pilot     14 rows   14 barriers   13 in vacuo   13 stabilisations
      extended  30 rows   28 barriers   22 in vacuo   22 stabilisations

## THE REGRESSION, WHICH IS THE HEADLINE RESULT
Comparator: Claeyssens et al. 2005, enzyme-only fit over 16 paths, read from their
Fig. 2(b) because the body text gives the gradient unsigned:  Y = 16.02 - 0.95 X, R = 0.97.

      set          n    gradient   in their sign   intercept      r    mean in vacuo
      pilot       13      +1.062          -1.062       19.08   +0.846        18.818
      extended    22      +0.998          -0.998       18.21   +0.933        18.222
      pooled      35      +1.029          -1.029       18.58   +0.893        18.443

THE EXTENDED ENSEMBLE REPRODUCES THE PUBLISHED FIT: gradient -0.998 against -0.95, and
r = +0.933 against R = 0.97, on 22 frames rather than the 8 available at the previous
HEAD. The agreement improved as the ensemble grew, which is the right direction.

## THE SIGN CONVENTION, WHICH MUST BE STATED EVERY TIME
stab_TS is defined here as barrier - barrier_vac, so NEGATIVE means the environment lowers
the barrier. Our mean is -4.79 kcal/mol over 21 frames.

Claeyssens define stabilisation with the OPPOSITE sign. Their means are +7.3 (2011) and
+4.2 (2005) kcal/mol. Same physics, opposite sign.

Consequently our gradient of +0.978 IS their -0.95. Comparing the raw signs would suggest
we had found the opposite relationship. The table above gives both columns for exactly
this reason, and the write-up must do the same wherever the gradient appears.

## THE INTERNAL CONSISTENCY TEST, WHICH THE EXTENDED SET PASSES BEST
Both papers state that the regression intercept should equal the mean in vacuo barrier,
because at zero stabilisation the enzyme barrier is the gas-phase one. Claeyssens 2005:
intercept 16.02 against a mean in vacuo of 16.2, a gap of 0.18.

      pilot     intercept 19.08   mean in vacuo 18.82   gap 0.27
      extended  intercept 18.21   mean in vacuo 18.22   gap 0.01
      pooled    intercept 18.58   mean in vacuo 18.44   gap 0.14

THE EXTENDED ENSEMBLE PASSES AT A GAP OF 0.01 kcal/mol, against 0.18 for the published
fit. This is not my inference about what the intercept ought to mean: both papers state it
themselves. Claeyssens 2005 calls the intercept "the predicted 'intrinsic' barrier from
the bound substrate conformation in the absence of TS stabilization", and 2011 adds that
it "is in good agreement with the gas phase barriers in both environments". The test is
theirs; we pass it more tightly than they do.

## WHY THE TWO SUB-ENSEMBLES MUST NOT BE POOLED
The pooled row above is computed for completeness and SHOULD NOT BE QUOTED. The two sets
differ in three ways, any one of which would be enough:

  1. SAMPLING WINDOW. Pilot frames come from before the equilibration cut; extended frames
     from after it. See deviation 6 on the 60 ns production.
  2. NAC CRITERION. Extended frames were selected against the full three-condition Hur &
     Bruice definition; the pilot set was selected on the distance clause alone and three
     of its members are labelled near_NAC as a result.
  3. RESTRAINT STRENGTH. The two sets were run with different scan spring constants. This
     is recorded in the project notes and is the reason the decision was taken that the
     extended set must reproduce Claeyssens on its own evidence.

IT DOES. That decision is vindicated: the extended eight give -0.978 and r = 0.929 without
help from the pilot set.

## DO NOT QUOTE
Frame 08170 carries a barrier of exactly 0.000000 and is labelled group=other. That is the
collapsed band, not a barrier of zero. It is excluded from every statistic computed here
and must be excluded from every statistic in the write-up. The check asserts its presence
and its signature so it cannot be silently reintroduced.

## Literature comparison, stated carefully
- THE GRADIENT REPRODUCES. -0.978 against -0.95, r 0.929 against 0.97, on an enzyme-only
  fit against an enzyme-only fit. This is the central positive result.
- THE INTERCEPT TEST PASSES, better than the published fit.
- THE MEAN BARRIER IS NOT YET COMPARABLE, and the write-up must not present it as if it
  were. Ours is computed at B3LYP-D3BJ/def2-SVP (deviation 9), from a freely relaxed
  reactant rather than a fixed r = -1.8 reference (deviation 3), on a single active site
  (deviation 7), from unrestrained reactant-state frames rather than TS-restrained ones
  (deviation 8). Four differences, each of which shifts a barrier. Claeyssens report
  11.3 +/- 1.8 (2011) and 12.0 +/- 1.7 (2005).
- THE SPREAD IS NOT THE SAME QUANTITY EITHER. Their standard deviation is across active
  site configurations from 16 paths; ours is within one site across a 60 ns trajectory.

So the defensible claim is about the RELATIONSHIP, not the absolute value: this work
reproduces the barrier-versus-stabilisation gradient of the published enzyme-only fit, on
an independently constructed system with a different force field, a different basis set
and a different sampling design. That is a stronger claim than matching a number, and it
is the one the evidence supports.

## Caveats and known issues
- THE ENSEMBLE IS NOT FINISHED. 28 of 30 extended frames have barriers and 22 have in
  vacuo references; 42 of 44 frames have a barrier. Two bands were still running at HEAD
  372fc3d. Every figure here is provisional and will move.
- THE IN VACUO REFERENCE IS AN ELECTRONICALLY UNBOUND DIANION, and this must be stated
  rather than left to be found. From refstate_diagnostic.pbs.out, recorded in
  SOLVATION_DECISION_NOTE.md:

      basis      environment      barrier    HOMO(R) Eh    bound?
      def2-SVP   vacuum            17.905    +0.082179      NO
      def2-SVP   CPCM eps = 4      17.474    -0.126365      yes
      def2-SVP   CPCM eps = 78.4   17.244    -0.192726      yes

  A positive HOMO means the highest occupied orbital lies above the vacuum level. Diffuse
  functions reduce it (+0.082 to +0.044 at def2-SVPD) but do not fix it.

  IT IS RETAINED, FOR TWO REASONS, BOTH CHECKABLE:
    1. The barrier is almost insensitive to it. Vacuum to bulk water moves the barrier by
       0.661 kcal/mol, despite the reactant itself being stabilised by -182 kcal/mol at
       eps = 78.4. The continuum stabilises reactant and transition state nearly equally,
       so the unboundedness cancels in the difference. That is also why a continuum cannot
       be a source of catalysis.
    2. CLAEYSSENS USED THE SAME REFERENCE, and said why. 2011 p. 6: "single-point
       calculations on the isolated QM region were carried out for the QM/MM optimized
       geometries ... calculating the energy of the reacting system without the effects of
       the protein or water environment". 2005: "We use the gas-phase profiles as a
       convenient and meaningful reference." The convention is inherited, not invented.
- The in vacuo references are single points at the optimised QM/MM geometries, not
  separately optimised gas-phase structures. That is the correct construction for a
  differential stabilisation, and it matches what Claeyssens do, but it should be stated.
- Frame directories under outputs/frames/ hold the per-frame reactant, TS and product QM
  geometries and the in vacuo inputs. The ORCA outputs themselves are not committed, so
  convergence per frame cannot be inspected from this repository.
