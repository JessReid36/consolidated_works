# 15_17_path_endpoints_scan — Reaction-path endpoints and the restrained scan

## Why these are one step
Step 15 optimises the reactant, step 16 drives a restrained scan along the reaction
coordinate, step 17 optimises the product. The scan is only interpretable against the two
endpoints, and the endpoints are only useful because the scan connects them. Splitting
them gives three folders none of which can be read alone.

## What this step did
Optimised both endpoints under QM/MM, then ran a 20-window restrained scan from
d(C4-O3) = 1.464 / d(C6-C1) = 3.251 to 2.400 / 1.540, i.e. nominally from r = -1.787 to
r = +0.860.

## Key numbers
- 20 windows, 20 energies, all on the QM/MM total scale (-1076.15 to -1076.10 Eh).
- Maximum on the reactant branch: window 11, r = -0.947, E = -1076.102940 Eh.
- Reactant-branch minimum: window 02, r = -1.705.
- Global minimum: window 20, r = +1.004, on the PRODUCT side.

## Acceptance threshold and verdict
Verification: 16 checks, 16 PASS, 0 FAIL. See checks/step15_17_verify.out.
The checks PASS by asserting the pathology below is present and correctly characterised.
That is deliberate: the point is to make it impossible to later quote the scan maximum as
a barrier without the check objecting.

## THE SCAN DOES NOT REACH THE TRANSITION STATE
Between windows 11 and 12 the achieved coordinate jumps from r = -0.947 to r = +0.416, a
gap of 1.363 A against a typical window step of 0.082 A. The system snapped over the
barrier.

CLAEYSSENS PLACE THE TRANSITION STATE AT r = -0.50, WHICH LIES INSIDE THAT GAP.

So the scan never samples the TS region at all. Its maximum, window 11, is the last point
before the snap - a terminus, not a stationary point. Any barrier read from it is a lower
bound on a quantity the scan cannot resolve.

This is the concrete justification for moving to NEB-TS, and it justifies the project's
existing rule that 1D relaxed-scan barriers are diagnostics and must never be quoted as
barriers. The rule is not caution; the scan demonstrably cannot do the job.

## THE RESTRAINTS DO NOT HOLD THE COORDINATE
Comparing achieved against target r, window by window:

      window   target r   achieved r      lag
        01       -1.787      -1.787     +0.000
        05       -1.230      -1.493     -0.263
        10       -0.534      -1.072     -0.538
        11       -0.393      -0.947     -0.554
        12       -0.254      +0.416     +0.670
        20       +0.860      +1.004     +0.144

On the reactant branch the lag grows monotonically to -0.554 A: the restraint pulls toward
the target and the optimiser never arrives. After the snap the sign flips, because the
system has fallen past the target.

Two consequences. The scan is not a controlled walk along the coordinate, so the window
labels do not mean what they appear to mean. And the lag is largest exactly where the
barrier is, so the scan is least trustworthy precisely where it matters.

## THE BARRIER DEPENDS ON THE REFERENCE, AND THE COMMONLY QUOTED VALUE IS DEFENSIBLE
      vs window 01 (r = -1.787)              16.59 kcal/mol
      vs reactant-branch minimum, window 02  17.58 kcal/mol
      vs global minimum, window 20           27.13 kcal/mol

The third is meaningless: window 20 is on the product side, so that number is
product-to-maximum.

The 16.6 kcal/mol that appears in the project notes uses window 01 as the reference. That
turns out to be the RIGHT choice for comparison with Claeyssens, and for a reason worth
stating: window 01 sits at r = -1.787 and Claeyssens' fixed reference state RS* is
r = -1.800. The two references differ by 0.013 A. So 16.6 is referenced almost exactly
where Claeyssens reference their barriers, which is a better-founded choice than it looks.
See protocol_deviations.txt deviation 3 on reference states.

Whichever is used, the reference must be stated alongside the number: the choice moves it
by a full kcal/mol.

## Literature comparison
Claeyssens et al. 2011 generate their profiles by adiabatic mapping along the same
coordinate and report that they converge no saddle point, taking instead "the highest
point" as the approximate TS structure, at r values between -0.5 and -0.7. Their approach
and ours share the same limitation, and they say so openly.

The difference is that their mapping resolves the region around r = -0.5; ours skips it
entirely. Their sampling is also restrained throughout at r = -0.3 during the MD that
generates starting structures, so their profiles begin from configurations already close
to the TS, whereas ours begin from a free reactant. That is deviation 8.

## Caveats and known issues
- The scan was run from a single frame. Nothing in this step is an ensemble result.
- reactant_opt.property.txt and product_opt.property.txt are committed but the ORCA output
  files themselves are not, so the optimisation convergence history cannot be inspected
  from this repository. Only the final property records survive.
- The scan targets file specifies both bond lengths per window, so the coordinate is
  driven as a pair of distance restraints rather than as r directly. That is why achieved
  r can lag: r is a derived quantity and nothing restrains it.
- No frequency analysis is possible on window 11, because it is not a stationary point.
  The TS characterisation belongs entirely to the NEB-TS work at step 18.
